Concept and Development

Cockatoo Expression Sheet

I began the piece by sketching out different ideas of what I could draw. It would be my first attempt to practice using the application Krita, so before I started I wanted an idea that I wanted to stick with. The concept for it developed after a small doodle I made in the corner of my brainstorming page. I chose it as I liked the idea of drawing a bird that was not the more 'classical' witch companion like a crow, owl, or raven.

My initial digital sketch(s) then looked like this as I chose early on to make the layout of the whole piece more a character/expression sheet for the bird, with just the added pose with the witch on the end. I did this so that more of the canvas could be utilized. When designing the witch I was inspired a bit by hawk/falcon trainers, which is why I added longer sleeves and gloves. The bird was based off of a sulphur-crested cockatoo, which was the parrot I thought best matched with the original pencil sketch I had started out with. After the digital sketches were done I decided on a general color scheme and started blocking out where I wanted the colors to go. Over time as I was drawing I altered the pose, outfit, and overal look of the witch to be less fierce/sharp and instead be designed with softer and rounder features to make her appear more friendly. I did this to match her more closely with the cockatoo (as they are considered highly social and expressive birds which would suit a more friendly looking witch.)
As I was still learning I discovered only after I completed the entire artwork that I could isolate layers which would allow me to shade them much more easily. Becasue of this I had instead made seperate shading layers for each individual piece/body part, and then organized multiple groups of those pieces. Which ended up resulting in lots of lagging and difficulty drawing. So out of everything I learned through this experince, the lesson I know will stick best with me is to merge groups to conserve file size, and to avoid making seperate shading layers when possible.

Throughout the process I learned a lot, by watching tutorials on the features in Krita and through simple trial and error until I made it work. In the end it was a fun experience as an intro to the application, as well as the opportunity to practice drawing a person (which is a subject I have wanted to practice more).

Spectator Clay Model

I modeled this 'Spectator' creature using air-dry modeling clay, 4 small wooden balls, and one plastic ping pong ball. The first thing I did was paint the ping pong ball so that it would make the painting process easier later. Afterwords I used it as a base to mold around and then added the additional eyestalks as that started drying. Once this part was completed, with a hole on the bottom in preparation, I then created the base. Each piece was dried and painted with acrylic paint before being attached together. The figure was not stable on its own, so a separate clear base was added to support it better. This was my first attempt to model a character from clay and, while I know many things could be improved, I am overall satisfied with how it turned out.
