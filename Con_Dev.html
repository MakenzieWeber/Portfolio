Concept and Development

Cockatoo Expression Sheet

I began the piece by sketching out different ideas of what I could draw. It would be my first attempt to practice using the application Krita, so before I started I wanted an idea that I wanted to stick with. The concept for it developed after a small doodle I made in the corner of my brainstorming page. I chose it as I liked the idea of drawing a bird that was not the more 'classical' witch companion like raven, crow, or an owl.

My initial digital sketch(s) then looked like this as I chose early on to make the layout of the whole piece more a character/expression sheet for the bird, with just the added pose with the witch on the end, so that more of the canvas could be utilized. I was inspired a bit by hawk/falcon trainers which is why I gave the witch longer sleeves and gloves. The bird was based off of a sulphur-crested cockatoo, which was the parrot I thought best matched with the pencil sketch I had started out with. After the digital sketches were done I decided on a color scheme and started blocking out where I wanted the colors to go. Over time as I was drawing I altered the pose and outfit of the witch to be less fierce/sharp and instead look more friendly to match with the cockatoo (as they are considered highly social and expressive birds which would suit a more expressive and friendly looking witch.)
As I was still learning I discovered only after I completed the entire artwork that I could isolate layers which would allow me to shade them much more easily. Becasue of this I had instead made seperate shading layers for each individual piece/body part, and then organized multiple groups of those pieces. Which ended up resulting in lots of lagging and difficulty drawing. So out of everything I learned through this experince, the lesson I know will stick best with me is to merge groups to conserve file size, and to avoid making seperate shading layers when possible.

Throughout the process I learned a lot, by watching tutorials on the features in Krita and through simple trial and error until I made it work. In the end it was a fun experience to practice using the newer application as well as practice in drawing a person, which is a subject I have wanted to practice more of.
